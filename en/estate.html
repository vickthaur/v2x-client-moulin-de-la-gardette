From one generation to the next

Moulin de la Gardette is passed down within the family. Today it is run by Jean-Baptiste Meunier, and his daughter Zoé is taking over.

Ten hectares, twenty-five plots, three red Gigondas and a rosé. The estate has been certified organic since 2012.

The wine is made and aged in the winery Jean-Baptiste built in 2003, then sold at the cellar door on the village square.

i.

The Gardette hill

A mill on the hill, vines since 1670

The estate takes its name from the old mill that once stood at the top of the Gardette hill, south of the village. The stony hillside has been known for its vines since 1670.

The name first appeared in 1958, when Geneviève Jurdic married Laurent Meunier. Since then the estate has passed from parents to children: to Jean-Baptiste in 1988, and now to Zoé.

The Gigondas vineyards, at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail.

ii.

The vineyard

Twenty-five plots, from 100 to 330 metres

The vines run from the plateau near the village up to the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail. Across ten hectares the soil changes often: Triassic marls, Miocene sands, limestone and clay.

Grenache dominates, and much of it is very old. The vines are fifty years old on average, and the oldest, which go into Ventabren, are close to a hundred. A little Syrah, Cinsault and Mourvèdre make up the rest of the plantings.

The estate has been certified organic since 2012. In 2018, a bad year for mildew, its higher vineyards were less affected.

Picking by hand.

iii.

The harvest

Late, by hand, often as whole bunches

Jean-Baptiste picks late. In 2019 he harvested in mid-October, nearly a month after most Gigondas estates.

Everything is picked by hand, and much of the crop is fermented as whole bunches. He is happy to wait for rain before picking: the tannins come out rounder, with no loss of freshness.

iv.

The winery

No added yeast, very little sulphur

Jean-Baptiste built the estate’s winery in 2003. Fermentations start on their own, with the yeasts on the grapes, and sulphur is used very sparingly.

La Petite Gardette spends about ten months in concrete tanks to keep its fruit. Tradition is aged for eighteen months in 228-litre Burgundy barrels, and Ventabren for twelve to eighteen months in foudres, large oak vats.

A year at the estate

From pruning to bottle

  1. Winter

    Pruning, plot by plot, across all twenty-five.

  2. Spring

    Working the soils, organically.

  3. Summer

    The Grenache ripens slowly on the slopes, between 100 and 330 metres.

  4. October

    A late harvest by hand, often as whole bunches.

  5. In the cellar

    Fermentation with native yeasts, then ten to eighteen months of ageing.

Taking over

Zoé, the next generation

Zoé, Jean-Baptiste’s eldest daughter, joined the estate after studying oenology and doing several placements at other wineries. Moulin de la Gardette is still handed down within the family.
