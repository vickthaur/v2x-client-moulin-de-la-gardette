The estate

A 10-hectare family estate on the Gardette slopes, south of the village. Jean-Baptiste Meunier makes three red Gigondas and a rosé here.

The Gardette hillside has been known for its vines since 1670. The estate’s name first appeared in 1958, when Geneviève Jurdic married Laurent Meunier. The mill itself once stood at the top of the hill.

Jean-Baptiste took over from his parents in 1988. The vineyards have been certified organic since 2012, and his eldest daughter, Zoé, has joined him at the estate.

The wines

Three red Gigondas, and a rosé

Hand-picked grapes, often fermented as whole bunches, with native yeasts and very little sulphur. What changes from one cuvée to the next is the age of the vines and the way each wine is aged.

  1. 01La Petite GardetteGigondas · 10-year-old vines, aged in tank→10years
  2. 02TraditionGigondas · 40-year-old vines, Burgundy barrels→40years
  3. 03VentabrenGigondas · the oldest vines, 80 to 100 years old→100years
  4. 04The roséSyrah, Cinsault, Grenache · whole bunches→Rosésteel

Available vintages and prices are given at the cellar door, over the phone or in reply to your order.

Terroir

From Triassic marls to Miocene sands

The twenty-five plots rise from 100 to 330 metres, from the plateau near the village to the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail. The soil changes from one plot to the next: marl, sand, limestone and clay. Grenache dominates, and much of it is very old.

  • Organic since 2012
  • Picked by hand
  • Native yeasts

The winemaker

Jean-Baptiste Meunier, and now Zoé

Before taking over the family estate in 1988, Jean-Baptiste worked in California, at Diamond Creek in the Napa Valley. He picks late and by hand, often after rain, which gives rounder tannins without any loss of freshness.

His eldest daughter, Zoé, joined him after studying oenology and doing several placements at other estates.
