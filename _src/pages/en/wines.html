Picked by hand, fermented with native yeasts, very little sulphur. What changes from one cuvée to the next is the age of the vines and the way each wine is aged.

Red Gigondas

La Petite Gardette

A juicy wine full of red fruit, with tannins that are present but light. Made to be drunk young.

Grape varieties
50% Grenache, 50% Syrah
Vines
The estate’s young vines, around ten years old
Winemaking
Hand-picked, fully destemmed, fermented in concrete tanks
Ageing
About ten months in concrete tanks, with no oak, to keep the fruit

Red Gigondas

Tradition

The estate’s wine for laying down, already being made in 1983. Its UK importer ranks it among the most elegant wines of the appellation.

Grape varieties
80% Grenache, 10% Mourvèdre, 10% Cinsault
Vines
Around forty years old, on marl and clay-limestone soils
Winemaking
Partly destemmed, concrete tanks, long maceration after fermentation
Ageing
Eighteen months in 228-litre Burgundy barrels, bottled unfined and unfiltered

Ventabren

The wine from the estate’s oldest Grenache vines, some of them a hundred years old. Ventabren means ‘where the wind blows’.

Writing for Decanter, Matt Walls named Moulin de la Gardette one of the ten most exciting estates in the southern Rhône.

Grape varieties
70% Grenache, 20% Syrah, 10% Cinsault
Vines
80 to 100 years old, the oldest on the estate
Ageing
Twelve to eighteen months in foudres, large oak vats
The name
‘Where the wind blows’

Winemaking
